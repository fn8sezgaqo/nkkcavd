Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy—popularly known as the "P-Shot"—has gained attention for treating erectile dysfunction (ED) and improving overall penile health. Below, we break down what you need to know, from basic science to real-world results.

What Is the P-Shot?

The Priapus Shot (P-Shot) uses your own blood plasma, concentrated with growth factors, to:

Stimulate tissue regeneration

Enhance blood flow to the penis

Potentially improve erection quality

By tapping into your body's natural healing mechanisms, the P-Shot aims to restore or boost sexual function without synthetic drugs or implants.

How PRP Works in Erectile Dysfunction

Blood Draw• A small blood sample (20–60 mL) is taken from your arm.

Centrifugation• Spinning the sample separates red blood cells from plasma rich in platelets and growth factors.

Activation• Platelets release proteins that prompt cell proliferation and new blood vessel formation.

Injection• Using fine needles, the clinician injects PRP into strategic areas of the penis.

These growth factors—VEGF, PDGF, TGF-β, among others—help rebuild micro-vascular networks and support healthier erectile tissue.

Who Is a Candidate?

Ideal candidates for PRP for erectile dysfunction generally include men who:

Experience mild to moderate ED

Have not had success with or wish to avoid oral medications (e.g., PDE5 inhibitors)

Seek a minimally invasive, drug-free option

Are in good overall health (no active infections or bleeding disorders)

PRP is not recommended for men with certain blood disorders, active cancers, or severe cardiovascular disease without medical clearance.

The P-Shot Procedure

Consultation• Review medical history, medications, and expectations.

Preparation• Clean and numb injection sites with topical anesthetic.

PRP Harvesting• Draw and process your blood to isolate PRP.

Injection• Administer several small injections into the penile shaft and head.

Observation• Monitor briefly for any immediate reactions.

Total in-office time: 30–60 minutes. Many men return to work the same day.

Benefits and Expected Results

Most men report gradual improvements over 4–12 weeks. Potential benefits include:

Firmer, more sustainable erections

Increased penile sensitivity

Improved sexual confidence and satisfaction

Possible enhancement of penile length or girth (limited data)

Results vary. Some may need a second session in 3–6 months to reinforce benefits. Maintenance treatments once a year are common.

Research and Evidence

Current research remains preliminary but promising:

Journal of Sexual Medicine (2015): 60% of mild-to-moderate ED patients saw satisfactory improvement after a single PRP session.

Urology Clinics review (2019): PRP showed safety and potential efficacy with minimal side effects.

American Urological Association (AUA) guidelines: Encourage ongoing clinical trials before broad recommendations.

Limitations of existing studies:

Small sample sizes

Short follow-up periods (6–12 months)

Lack of large-scale randomized controlled trials

While evidence supports safety, definitive proof of long-term efficacy requires further high-quality research.

Risks and Side Effects

PRP uses your own blood, minimizing the risk of allergic reactions. Possible side effects include:

Mild pain or discomfort at injection sites

Temporary bruising or swelling

Minor bleeding

Very low risk of infection (standard sterile technique mitigates this)

Most side effects resolve within a few days. Serious complications are rare when performed by qualified providers.

Recovery and Aftercare

Avoid sexual activity, vigorous exercise, or heavy lifting for 48–72 hours.

Apply ice packs or take over-the-counter pain relief if needed.

Stay hydrated and follow any specific instructions from your clinician.
