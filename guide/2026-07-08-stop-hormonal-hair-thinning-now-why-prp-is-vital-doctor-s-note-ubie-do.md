Explanation

Hormonal hair thinning affects many women at different stages of life. Changes in estrogen, progesterone and androgens (male hormones) can disrupt the natural hair growth cycle. Platelet‐Rich Plasma (PRP) has emerged as a promising, minimally invasive treatment. In this article, we'll explain hormonal hair thinning, how PRP for hair loss for women works, who is a good candidate, what to expect, and when to seek professional help.

Hormonal Hair Thinning in Women: What You Need to Know

Hair grows in cycles: a growth phase (anagen), a transition phase (catagen), and a resting phase (telogen), followed by shedding. Hormonal imbalances can shorten the anagen phase, prolong telogen, or cause follicles to shrink (miniaturization), leading to:

Thinning at the crown, part line or temples

Overall decrease in hair volume

Noticeable shedding on pillow or in shower drain

Common Hormonal Triggers

Androgenic Alopecia (Female Pattern Hair Loss)Sensitivity to dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a potent androgen, can shrink follicles.

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)Elevated androgens and insulin resistance often cause diffuse thinning.

Thyroid DisordersBoth hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism disrupt hair cycling.

Perimenopause & MenopauseDeclining estrogen and progesterone can unbalance hair growth.

Postpartum ChangesA sudden drop in estrogen after childbirth can trigger telogen effluvium (temporary shedding).

Recognizing the Signs

You may be experiencing hormonal hair thinning if you notice:

A gradually widening part or thinning crown

Excessive hair on your brush, clothes or drain

Slower regrowth after shedding

Scalp more visible than before

Diagnosing Hormonal Hair Thinning

A thorough assessment by a qualified healthcare provider typically includes:

Medical History & SymptomsReview of menstrual patterns, medications, family history.

Physical ExamInspection of scalp, hair pull test, distribution of thinning.

Blood TestsThyroid function (TSH, T4), androgen levels (testosterone, DHEA-S), ferritin, vitamin D.

Scalp Imaging (if needed)Dermoscopy to view follicle health and miniaturization.

Accurate diagnosis guides targeted treatment. If labs reveal a serious or life‐threatening issue (e.g., severe thyroid imbalance), seek immediate medical care.

Treatment Options Overview

Hormonal hair thinning often responds best to a combination of therapies:

Topical MinoxidilIncreases blood flow to follicles; may cause initial shedding.

Anti‐androgens (e.g., spironolactone, oral contraceptives)Block DHT or reduce ovarian androgen production.

Nutritional SupportIron, biotin, vitamin D, zinc—only if lab‐proven deficient.

Low‐Level Laser TherapyPhotobiomodulation to stimulate follicles.

Platelet‐Rich Plasma (PRP)Uses your platelets to promote growth factors at the follicle level.

PRP Preparation & Injection Process

Blood Draw10–20 mL of your blood is collected.

CentrifugationSpins separate red cells, platelet‐poor plasma and platelet-rich plasma.

Activation (optional)Some protocols add calcium chloride or thrombin to activate platelets.

Scalp InjectionMicroinjections of PRP are administered into areas of thinning.

Most clinics complete the process in 45–60 minutes. Topical numbing cream can minimize discomfort.

Risks and Side Effects

PRP is generally safe. Possible side effects include:

Mild pain or bruising at injection sites

Temporary redness or swelling

Headache or dizziness (rare)

Serious complications (infection, injury to blood vessels or nerves) are extremely uncommon when performed by trained professionals.
