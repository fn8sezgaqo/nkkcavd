Can lower abdominal pain and testicle pain be related to a hernia?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Lower abdominal and testicle pain are common warning signs of an inguinal hernia, a condition where soft tissue — often part of the intestine — pushes through a weak spot in the lower abdominal muscles. This can create a visible bulge in the groin and cause discomfort that worsens with coughing, lifting, or standing for long periods.

Key symptoms of an inguinal hernia include:

[May Be Hernia](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/)

A bulge in the groin or scrotum

Aching, burning, or heaviness in the lower abdomen

[May Be Hernia Act](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/1081317307.html)

Pain or pressure in the testicle area

Discomfort that intensifies with physical activity

While some hernias are mild, others can become serious if the tissue gets trapped (incarcerated) or loses blood supply (strangulated), which requires emergency care.

Because groin and testicle pain can also point to other conditions — like a urinary tract infection, kidney stone, or testicular issue — it's important to identify the cause quickly. Explanation

An inguinal hernia occurs when tissue, like part of the intestine, pushes through a weak spot in the abdominal muscles. This can cause pain in the lower abdomen and sometimes in the testicles.

Signs of an Inguinal Hernia
