Common causes include trigeminal neuropathy (irritation or damage to the trigeminal nerve), sinus infections or inflammation pressing on facial nerves, and blood vessel conditions such as stroke or migraine that disrupt normal nerve signaling.

Because facial numbness can range from a minor nerve irritation to a medical emergency like stroke, identifying the cause quickly matters. Feeling numbness on the left side of your face can be due to several reasons. One possible cause is trigeminal neuropathy, which affects the nerve responsible for sensation in the face. This condition can lead to numbness or tingling.

Another potential cause is the Silent Sinus Syndrome, which involves changes in the sinuses that can lead to facial numbness. This condition is less common but can cause noticeable changes in facial sensation.

Lastly, issues with blood vessels, such as changes in the carotid artery, can also cause facial numbness. These changes can affect how nerves function and lead to sensations like numbness or tingling.
