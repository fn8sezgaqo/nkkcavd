Internal Medicine

Olfactory hallucinations, also known as phantosmia, occur when a person perceives smells that aren't actually present. These phantom odors can range from pleasant scents to unpleasant ones like smoke, burning, or rotting. Olfactory hallucinations are sometimes associated with schizophrenia and often occur alongside other sensory hallucinations, such as auditory or visual disturbances. They may also signal neurological conditions, migraines, seizures, or sinus issues.

Because phantom smells can point to a wide range of causes—from minor to serious—it's important not to guess.
