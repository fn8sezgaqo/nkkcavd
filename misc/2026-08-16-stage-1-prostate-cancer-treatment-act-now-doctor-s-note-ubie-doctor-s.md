What is the typical treatment approach for stage 1 prostate cancer?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Stage 1 prostate cancer is typically low-risk and highly treatable. The most common management options include:

Active surveillance: Regular monitoring with PSA tests, exams, and biopsies to track any changes.

Surgery (radical prostatectomy): Removal of the prostate gland, often recommended for younger or healthier patients.

Radiation therapy: External beam radiation or brachytherapy to target cancer cells.

The right choice depends on your age, overall health, life expectancy, and personal preferences. Because Stage 1 tumors grow slowly, many patients live long, healthy lives with minimal intervention.

1. Active Surveillance:

This approach involves closely monitoring the cancer without immediate treatment. Regular check-ups, including PSA tests and biopsies, help track the cancer's progress. It is often chosen if the cancer is growing slowly and not causing symptoms.

2. Surgery:

Surgery, specifically a prostatectomy, is the removal of the prostate gland. It is considered for patients who are healthy enough for surgery and prefer a more immediate treatment.

3. Radiation Therapy:

Radiation therapy uses high-energy rays to target and kill cancer cells. It can be delivered externally or through implanted seeds (brachytherapy). This option is suitable for those who prefer not to undergo surgery.

Each of these treatments has its own benefits and risks, and the choice depends on the patient's preferences, age, and overall health. The goal is to manage the cancer effectively while minimizing side effects.
