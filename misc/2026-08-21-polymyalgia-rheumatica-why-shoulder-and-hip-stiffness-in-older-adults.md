Polymyalgia Rheumatica: Why Shoulder and Hip Stiffness in Older Adults Warrants Immediate Testing

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) is an inflammatory condition that causes sudden stiffness and pain in the shoulders, neck, and hips, primarily affecting adults over age 50. Common symptoms include morning stiffness lasting more than 45 minutes, bilateral aching, low-grade fever, fatigue, and unintended weight loss.

Early diagnosis matters because PMR responds well to low-dose corticosteroids, but prompt evaluation is essential to rule out giant cell arteritis—a serious complication that can cause permanent vision loss—and to distinguish PMR from similar conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, fibromyalgia, and hypothyroidism. Diagnosis typically involves blood tests (ESR and CRP) plus a thorough clinical evaluation.

Because PMR symptoms overlap with many other conditions and delayed treatment can lead to serious complications, identifying what's driving your symptoms quickly is critical. What Is Polymyalgia Rheumatica?

Definition: A systemic inflammatory disorder leading to muscle pain and stiffness.

Adults older than 50

Slightly more common in women than men

More prevalent in people of Northern European descent

Onset: Often develops over days to weeks, not gradually over months

Why Immediate Testing Matters

Speedy Symptom Relief

Prompt diagnosis allows for early corticosteroid therapy, often reducing pain within days.

Preventing Complications

Untreated PMR can lead to prolonged disability and reduced quality of life.

Up to 15% of PMR patients develop giant cell arteritis (GCA), a serious blood vessel inflammation that can cause vision loss.
