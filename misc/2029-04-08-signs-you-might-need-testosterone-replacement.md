Common Signs of Low Testosterone

Low testosterone can affect your physical, emotional, and sexual health. Common signs of low testosterone include decreased muscle mass, increased body fat, fatigue, mood swings, depression, irritability, reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, and decreased semen volume.

Physical Symptoms

Low testosterone can lead to some noticeable physical changes. One of the main symptoms is decreased muscle mass. You might find it challenging to build or maintain muscle despite regular exercise. Also, increased body fat is a common sign, especially around the abdomen, even if your diet and activity levels remain consistent.

Fatigue and decreased energy levels may also be an indication. You may feel unusually tired throughout the day, lacking the energy for activities you once enjoyed. This ongoing fatigue can affect your overall quality of life and reduce your productivity levels.

What Happens If a Man Has Low Testosterone?

Untreated testosterone deficiencies can pose a significant health risk. If left unaddressed, the health risks of low testosterone can lead to serious physical and mental health issues.

Physically, low testosterone can result in decreased bone density, increasing the risk of fractures and osteoporosis. Individuals with low testosterone levels may also experience muscle loss and increased body fat, which can contribute to obesity and related health problems like type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

Mentally, untreated low testosterone can cause severe mood swings, depression, and cognitive decline. If you have diminished testosterone levels, you may find yourself struggling with concentration, memory, and overall mental clarity.

Diagnosing Low Testosterone

To determine if your testosterone is low, you can start by increasing your awareness of some of the common symptoms that we have discussed such as fatigue, mood swings, decreased muscle mass, and reduced libido. If you experience these signs, you should immediately consult with a healthcare provider.

Diagnosing low testosterone involves a thorough medical consultation and a testosterone blood test. Your doctor will review your symptoms, your medical history, and perform a physical exam. The testosterone blood test, typically done in the morning when levels are highest, measures the amount of testosterone in your blood.

Treatment Options

Treating low testosterone often involves testosterone replacement therapy (TRT).5 TRT can help restore testosterone levels through various methods such as injections, gels, patches, and pellets. This therapy can help alleviate symptoms like fatigue, decreased muscle mass, and reduced libido, which can go a long way toward improving your overall quality of life.

Aside from TRT, other potential methods that may boost your testosterone levels include lifestyle changes.6 Regular exercise, a balanced diet, adequate sleep, and stress management can naturally boost testosterone levels. Certain supplements may also help support hormone levels.

Lifestyle Changes and Natural Remedies

To raise testosterone naturally, consider making specific lifestyle changes. A balanced diet rich in proteins, healthy fats, and carbohydrates is essential for restoring testosterone levels, and foods like lean meats, nuts, and whole grains all act as natural testosterone boosters.

Regular exercise, especially weightlifting and high-intensity interval training (HIIT), can significantly boost testosterone levels. You should aim for a minimum of 30 minutes of physical activity most days of the week. Supplements such as vitamin D and zinc can also help bridge nutritional gaps and support healthy testosterone levels. Always talk to your healthcare provider before starting any supplement regimen.
