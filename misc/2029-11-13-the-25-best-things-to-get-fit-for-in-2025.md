# The 25 Best Things to Get Fit for in 2025

PRETTY MUCH NOBODY gets revved to get out the door for a workout “to get healthy.” You need a thing: One that challenges you, terrifies you a little bit, hands you your ass, or creates a major sense of FOMO when your friends are getting fit, posting the pics and having a great time.

There’s a whole body of research on how goals work for you, but the bottom line from landmark research more than 50 years ago still holds: Big goals lead to greater effort than low-hanging ones, and specific and challenging ones drive you to perform better. Add friends, and you sweeten the pot. New research from a team including Katy Milkman, Ph.D., author of How to Change, confirms what you pretty much already knew: That working out with others keeps you accountable so you do it more and like it better.

So this year, get something on the calendar—something like the events below that bring out your competitive side, are epic enough to make you really reach for bragging rights, or ones that bring you tighter with a group. Use this list to get stoked and get started.

Turf Wars

June 21; $250

Want to test your conditioning training? Turf Wars, an event sponsored by the legendary training facility Tone House, is a next-level fitness competition in New York City. Compete by yourself or in a pair through 10 events, ranging from sprints, dumbbell thrusters, assault bike, and sandbag clean and presses. Bonus: The entry fee includes a full Adidas outfit and shoes.

Green Beret Fitness, Operation One Zero

July 12; $65.

[to Get](https://47466.com/oroud1jlz/)

Go rucking with Operation One Zero in Provo, Utah. This beginner-friendly race-with-weight has you navigate your through a 10-mile course along the Provo River. As the trek goes on, the challenge grows to be just as much of a mental as a physical one. Or, pull your weight through Green Beret Fitness events across the country, designed to push the kind of strength that’s built in military training. This group has races for all abilities all over the country, year round.

Ötillö SwimRun

$85 to $600 per athlete depending on distance

Conquer this race by land and sea: SwimRun is all about swimming to a land mass, running over it to the next bit of water, then repeating it again and again. Born from the original Ötillö, the tough endurance race that covers 24 islands in the Swedish archipelago (now the SwimRun world championships, with 38 miles of running and 5.6 miles of swimming), the brand now offers a series of SwimRun races in the U.S. (and world) at a range of distances. Why not start with the Casco Bay, Maine race—the site of the first official American SwimRun? Choose from total distances of about 10, 17, and 35 miles; the classic way to do these is with a partner.

IU Health 500 Festival Mini Marathon

May 3; $89.

Pound the same pavement legends Mario Andretti, Al Unser, and A.J. Foyt once dominated. Find your top speed along the course of the IU Health 500 Festival Mini Marathon in Indiana, where you complete a lap on the Indianapolis Motor Speedway before crossing the finishing line in downtown Indy. May 3; indymini.com; $89.

Hyrox

Multiple locations and dates; ~$125

So, you don’t fancy running long distances or sitting on a bike for hundreds of miles, but you still want to compete. If wall balls, burpees, farmer’s carries, and sled pushes are more your style, then look no further than Hyrox, the competition that mixes running and functional training into an 8-event competition that will test your strength, speed, and endurance.

GO EPIC

NOTHING GETS YOU training on days you don’t want to like having a big hairy challenge on the calendar. Get your adrenaline going with these.

Oahu Pentathlon

August 9; $156-$332

If you’re bored by one sport—or even three—the Oahu Pentathlon is the ultimate versatility challenge. Start at sunrise and circumnavigate the island’s 135 miles with a 2-mile swim, 16-mile run, 12-mile paddle, 10-mile carry, and 95-mile bike—by sunrise the next morning. Go solo or divide it into a team event.

Grand Canyon Rim to Rim Run

September 19-22 and 26-29; $1995 + $2,000 in fundraising

Quad-busting downhills. Extreme temperature shifts. Narrow ledges. Nearly 5,000 feet of elevation change each way. The iconic Grand Canyon rim-to-rim run offers a 20.6- or 23-miler, depending on which way you go, and takes in-shape runners 8 to 13 hours. Next Level: Go rim-to-rim-to-rim in a day. Most people do it DIY; you can also raise money for charity via Run2Revive and do it as a group.

Fjallraven Classic

July, 2025; $200

The 3-day, 30.5-mile Fjallraven Classic Trek in the Colorado Rockies is solid, eco-friendly and no-nonsense. Fjallraven provides maps and water stops while you go at your own pace and carry everything you need from 9,000 feet to almost 12,000 and back through untamed territory. You’ll need to be fit, but the reasonable daily mileage means slower trekkers can join, too. Limited spots mean you’re only sharing trails with about 80 people.

Summit World's Tallest Climbing Wall

$35 for an hour of climbing

If you have a fear of heights, you can skip over this part. The world’s tallest artificial climbing wall towers 140 feet over the Historic Banning Mills in Whitesburg, Georgia (for comparison, an average indoor top-rope climbing wall is about 30 feet high). It has 9 climbing lanes that offer challenges to both beginners and experts.

Pittsburgh Dirty Dozen

October, 2025; $65-$75

Test your hill training by completing 13 (baker’s dozen) of the city’s toughest climbs in the Pittsburgh Dirty Dozen bike race. Shortish ones (1/4 mile) offer no mercy with 13- to 20-percent grades. The race caters to both mountain goats and mere mortals with categories for serious racers and more casual competitors.

Stubborn Mule Adventure Race

$80 to $250, depending on event length

Challenge your strategy skills, map-and-compass navigation, and endurance across land and water in this bike, trek, and paddle adventure race. Your ability to make decisions and roll with uncertainty also gets put to the test: The event order isn’t revealed until race day. Sign up for a 5-hour, 12-hour, or 24-hour option; the disciplines are the same but the distances change. Tackle the course as a team of up to four or do it on your own (with race director permission).

GET TOGETHER

ONE OF THE best ways to get in shape: Have a workout buddy. Grab your training partner (or partners) and work toward something together with these team-oriented feats.

Special Olympics Plane Pull

Various locations; around $1,000 per team

Assemble a team of your fellow back-day enthusiasts to heft a 140,000-pound Boeing 737 across a tarmac as fast as you can, in support of Special Olympics. Pro tip: Practice on a sled, first.

The Gauley Marathon

$300 per person

Paddle your way through class three to class five rapids on one of the most legendary whitewater rivers in the world with a handful of fellow adrenaline junkies—all in one day. With West Virginia Adventure's guidance, you’ll battle your way through 26 miles of fast flooding water, through the Upper and Lower sections of the Gauley river in the nation's newest National Park. Don't go in green for this endeavor, though—gain some experience by completing other whitewater raft tours before hopping into this legendary marathon.

Pro tip: go the same weekend as the legendary Bridge Day, and watch people base jump off the New River Gorge Bridge after your adventure.

Hood to Coast Relay

August 22-23; $1,776 to $2,483 per team, depending on distance

Test your running and walking endurance with eight to 12 of your fittest buddies in the “Mother of all Relays,” the Providence Hood to Coast Relay. The 41-year-old racecourse leads teams through the Oregon backroads from the base of Mount Hood (198 miles) or Portland (128 miles) all the way to the coastline.

Pinot Paddle

Mid-July; $500 per person

You’re going to want to prioritize arm day and core training to make it through Pinot Paddle, a two-day river ride through Oregon’s Willamette Valley. Don’t worry, there are wine tastings along the way.

DO IT DIY

YOU DON'T HAVE to join an official event to have fun or go epic. Challenge yourself (and friends!) to these do-it-yourself feats that match your schedule.

Everesting

Find a hill and climb it. Then climb it again, and again, and again until you’ve climbed the height of Everest—29,032 feet. All in one go. No sleep. Same sport the whole way. If you crush it before it crushes you, you might even get your name in the hall of fame on Everesting.com. According to the creators of the concept and founders of the site, the most common way to Everest is by bike, but people have run it, hiked it, skied it and even walked that height up and down stairs. So far, there have been more than 31,000 successful Everestings in 116 countries. Are you next? Make your own way (and document it!) or join an Everesting official event.

Presidential Traverse

Summit seven mountains in one (tough) hike in New Hampshire. The mountains, named for former presidents (Madison, Adams, Jefferson, Washington, Monroe, Eisenhower, Pierce), include the five tallest in the Northeastern US. Climb about 10,000 vertical feet over 19 to 24 miles. Most people do it in two or more days; make it an ultra challenge (if you’re ready for it!) and do it all in one.

Host Your Own NFL Combine

[to Get Fit](https://7161777.com/nr3os91esr/)

Need a few ways to determine your fantasy draft order? Host your own NFL combine. Grab a handful of friends, some equipment, and run through the legendary test of agility, strength, and speed. The combine includes 7 events: 40-yard dash, bench press, broad jump, vertical jump, shuttle run, and the three-cone drill.
