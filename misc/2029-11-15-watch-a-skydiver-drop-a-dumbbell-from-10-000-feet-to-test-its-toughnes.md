# Watch a Skydiver Drop a Dumbbell From 10,000 Feet to Test Its Toughness

If you're planning to snap up some strength training equipment for your home gym, one of your top concerns is going to be durability. Weights aren't cheap, so before you plunk down some of your hard-earned cash for gear, you're going to want to make sure it can stand up to the rough and tumble nature of your toughest workouts. You probably feel fine tossing around equipment at a gym since you pay a membership fee for the privilege, but at home, when you have to foot the bill to replace every plate and weight, you might be a little more cautious if you think the next failed rep could take your gear out of commission.

[Dumbbell From Feet to](https://pepenails.com/8kvefi/)

One gear brand went to the furthest levels of extreme to prove its equipment can stand up to your toughest workouts, then recorded the whole next-level stress test for a flashy YouTube video for good measure. Tru Grit Fitness, which produces everything from weight racks and dumbbells to cardio machines, enlisted Red Bull Air Force member and skydiving athlete Jeff Provenzano to take one of the brand's 45-pound dumbbells up 10,000 feet in a helicopter. The stunt wasn't just for Provenzano to haul the weight up into the air for nothing, though—after he jumped from the aircraft clutching the dumbbell, he let it go to fall to Earth.

According to the brand, "several weights of dumbbells" were from released from "various altitudes between 8-10,000 feet." The stress test completed in November 2021 in Eloy, Arizona, which Tru Grit says is home to the world’s largest skydive dropzone.

But the drop was only half the test. To prove that the gear could handle the extreme abuse, elite CrossFit competitor and Titan Games winner Dani Speegle was on the ground to collect the weight and perform a dumbbell snatch on the sandy desert floor. There's no clear, unedited cut of the weight landing and Speegle picking it up in the clip, so we'll have to take the brand at their word that the dumbbell stayed intact. Still, the whole wild stunt shows that in the pursuit of getting a good workout with solid gear, there's no limit to the heights some people will go.
