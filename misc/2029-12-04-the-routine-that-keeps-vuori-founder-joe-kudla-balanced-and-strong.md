# The Routine That Keeps Vuori Founder Joe Kudla Balanced and Strong

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

THE SUN IS RISING on the California coast as Joe Kudla stares out at the Pacific Ocean, a surfboard under his arm. The days are busy for the founder and CEO of the activewear brand Vuori, recently valued at $5.5 billion, and Kudla is cherishing this moment of peace. Chasing waves has a calming effect on his mind and does wonders to activate his body.

“My mornings have become a sacred time for me,” he says. He wakes before dawn in his home in the beach community of Leucadia, just 10 minutes from Vuori’s offices in Carlsbad. This lets him make the most of the quiet hours before his two daughters rise. Kudla spends this time doing a short Kelee meditation, then journals or dips into a classic book on philosophy or self-improvement. Lately he’s been rereading chapters of "The Daily Stoic" by Ryan Holiday and "The Greatest Salesman in the World" by Og Mandino, which he picked up after hearing Matthew McConaughey speak about it on a podcast.

For the 47-year-old Kudla, taking care of his mind is crucial—for both his own wellness and the wellness of Vuori. He has big long-term goals: Professionally, he wants to continue growing Vuori, and personally, he’s trying to summit all the 14,000-foot peaks in Cali (right now, he’s halfway through). “Great companies are the accumulation of good decisions made on a consistent basis over a long period of time,” he says. His daily habits prep him to think clearly when the first work email arrives. “I think clarity is the biggest currency in entrepreneurship, and I consider these practices to be early investments in that clarity I need.”

Kudla’s investments in himself extend beyond the surfboard. Once he returns to his living room, he keeps maximizing his early active time, working through stretches, yoga, and dynamic neuromuscular stabilization (DNS) drills. Kudla adopted DNS, which blends planks and breathing drills to create total-body awareness, a few years ago, on the advice of Mike Rintala, DC, a DNS disciple, and surfer Taylor Knox. The gentle movement helps alleviate Kudla’s back pain, an artifact of years of high school football. “I found warming up my body like this, doing a little light meditation and something active, really sets the tone for my day,” he says.

Not that Kudla is only about chill movement. Sometimes he opts to hit the trails near his home on his hybrid Specialized mountain bike with a couple of friends. Or he heads to the gym in his Carlsbad office for a workout with his longtime trainer, Kevin Lewallen. Here, Kudla blends muscle moves like rows with balance-focused moves on a BOSU ball. “It’s about building the stability and strength I need to do the things I love,” he says. And from summiting mountains to riding waves, all the athletic pursuits supercharge him. “These moments out in the water help me stay connected with our original inspiration and my body,” Kudla says. “They make me a better leader, a better entrepreneur, a better husband, and a better father.”
