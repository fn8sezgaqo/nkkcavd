Why would a man want this testing done in addition to testosterone testing?

Many men may test within the normal range for total testosterone but still feel like absolute garbage!

Why? Well, the standard range used by most clinics for testosterone testing is based on results obtained from men of varying health backgrounds and ages.

This leads to a reference range that may not accurately represent what a man’s testosterone levels should be as men who are older or have certain conditions typically have lower testosterone values that set the normal range much lower than it would be if results only reflected values obtained from healthy, younger men.

Thus, men who want to see a somewhat bigger picture of their health may choose to add on sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) and free androgen index (FAI).

SHBG

SHBG binds itself to testosterone and controls how much of it is delivered to the body’s tissues.

Thus, while total testosterone shows how much testosterone is in a man’s body the SHBG test indicated how much of this testosterone is actually being used by the body (not bound to SHBG) which can help confirm androgen deficiency.

A normal range for SHBG is generally considered to be 16–55 nmol/L for men ages 20-49.

This reference range increases to 19.3−76.4 nmol/L for men who are 50+, indicating that less testosterone is available for use by the body.

FAI

Free Androgen Index can also be used towards estimating physiologically active testosterone vs total testosterone and helps to further determine male sex hormone status.

It is obtained by dividing total testosterone by SHBG (in units of nmol/L) and multiplying this value by 100.

A standard range for FAI is 24–104 by Lets Get Checked’s clinical standards, however, the following age-dependent reference ranges are also used by some labs:

30.0–128.0 for ages 0-29

24.0–122.0 for ages 30-39

14.0-126.0 for ages 40-49

18.0-82.0 for ages 50+

Oestradiol

Oestradiol (also referred to as estradiol/E2) is one of the 3 main forms of estrogen that plays a role in libido, erectile function, and spermatogenesis in men.

It is found in much smaller concentrations in men than women but men, however, men with extremely low levels of E2 may be susceptible to accelerated bone loss, aromatase inhibitor deficiency, and impairments in major physiologic functions.

The reference interval for estradiol in adult men is 27.9-156.4 pmol/L which converts to 7.6−42.6 pg/mL.

Prolactin

Although prolactin is best known for its role in breast milk production in women, it also dictates testosterone secretion and spermatogenesis.

The reference internal for prolactin in men provided by Lets Get Checked is 86–324 mIU/L.
