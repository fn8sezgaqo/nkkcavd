When news broke that Robert Pattinson would be the next actor to take on the role of DC's Batman, fans were curious to see what the movie would look like, and teasers of Pattinson in the suit and The Batmobile just piqued interest even further.

This past weekend, the first full teaser trailer for the movie was released, and fans got to see a first glimpse at one of Pattinson's stunning fight scenes, first shots of Zoë Kravitz as Catwoman, and Colin Farrell's transformation into Penguin.

Another aspect of the trailer that also caught fans' attention is the Nirvana song that plays during the two and a half minute teaser. "Something in the Way" is the last track on the band's 1991 album Nevermind, and while The Batman's composer Michael Giacchino takes the track and slightly alters it with orchestral flourishes, it still stays pretty similar to the original, as Loudwire explains.

As you can expect, both fans of the band and the upcoming movie have been sharing their thoughts about the song's inclusion in the trailer online:

And while most of the responses have been positive, there have also been a number of fans that believe Kurt Cobain wouldn't have wanted any of Nirvana's songs to be used in a movie trailer:

As ComicBook.com explains, the song was likely chosen because of its parallel's to Bruce Wayne's life:

"'Something In The Way' paints a picture of an individual living underneath a bridge, which many of thought was in reference to Cobain's own life having reportedly been homeless for a brief time. The chorus, a repetition of the title, can be seen as being in reference to the narrator's time under the bridge, a part of his life that he cannot seem to get over. This should sound familiar to Batman fans, as Bruce Wayne's entire mission is driven by the pain and trauma he feels for his parents death as a child."

The Batman is expected to open on October 1, 2021, and it also stars Paul Dano as The Riddler, Andy Serkis as Alfred, Peter Sarsgaard as a mystery character and John Turturro as crime boss Carmine Falcone.
