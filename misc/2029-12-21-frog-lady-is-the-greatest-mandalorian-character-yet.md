Star Wars fans have universally embraced Frog Lady.

After a first episode filled with sand dragons and cool ass space marshals, The Mandalorian veered away from its central story in Episode 2 to instead focus on what was, essentially, an intergalactic ride share adventure (Maybe Jon Favreau and director Peyton Reed really liked Stuber?). Mando—still looking to find other Mandalorians—was instead tasked with giving someone a ride through an Alien-esque, Spider creature-filled land. That someone ended up being an amphibious looking space creature known and identified only in episode subtitles as Frog Lady.

Frog Lady, a lady who appears to also be a large frog, had the unenviable task of transporting her space eggs across the galaxy to keep her line of life alive. Throughout the episode, we see this Frog Lady (Misty Rosas, who performed as Kuill in Season 1 while Nick Nolte provided the voice) embark on quite the journey. At first, she was simply buckled in and expecting to get a ride from point A to point B, but she ends up in a chase, screaming bloody murder, flying out of her seat, after Mando essentially resists his routine pull-over from what was basically the guy from Kim's Convenience pulling him over for a busted tail light (an excellent maneuver by Mando, we might add).

Part of the glory of Frog Lady, of course, is that she never got a name. She must have a name, clearly— she is able to fully communicate an intelligent message using the discarded parts of the combat droid who was in league with outlaw Bill Burr and company last season—but we never get it. It's not important. Frog Lady is good. Frog Lady will do.

It's worth considering, too, that we're not alone: quite literally no one in the show knows what Frog Lady's name is. Mando only even crosses paths with our newly beloved Frog Lady because of a deal requested by the Tatooine space mechanic Peli Motto (Amy Sedaris), who only met Frog Lady 10 minutes prior (she is an excellent judge of character, she promises, despite also seemingly not knowing Ms. Frog Lady's name).

Frog Lady, we quickly learn, is pure. She is perfect. She has done nothing wrong. And yet for her trouble, she comes out of the deal with significantly less of her inventory then when she started. That's because Baby Yoda, The Child, whatever you want to call that little rascal, decided to EAT the Frog Lady's young. Multiple times!

[the Greatest Mandalorian Character](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/)

Adorable, yes, and, sure, who doesn't love a nice egg breakfast? But this is our dear little Baby Yoda eating the eggs of our new friend, the venerable Frog Lady. It seems by the end of the episode that Frog Lady will be OK, even with many of her youth digested by the small green one. But mark our words—Frog Lady will rise. She will get what she wants, she will get what she needs, and more. It's hard to say that Baby Yoda will see revenge, because who wants that, but maybe he'll just get a little upset stomach. Something to really learn his lesson and come away knowing that eating other people's offspring is not OK.

With a weekend that was filled with worldwide news, it feels like there was a little bit smaller of a Mandalorian turnout than usual. People were distracted, and didn't necessarily seem to carve out the same 40 minutes into their schedules as normal. But with all the headlines, it's good to know that anyone who clicked onto The Mandalorian, longtime Star Wars fan or not, was treated to the first ever appearance of the one, the only, and the now legendary Frog Lady. Their loyalty was rewarded.

A quick glimpse through Twitter shows that fans by and large have the same feeling about Frog Lady; not the hero we necessarily deserved, but the hero we truly needed. A true driving force of good.

Although the force of Baby Yoda did, at times, overwhelm the force of Frog Lady. Some Tweets expertly pointed this out.

Very late in the first season of The Mandalorian, we learned that our as-to-that-point-unnamed hero did, in fact, have a name: and it was Din Djarin. In some ways, that provided relief: we learned that the character under the helmet and armor was no one we had ever heard of or seen before. Like The Last Jedi tried to prove before it was retconned by The Rise of Skywalker, truly anyone could be an important piece of the Star Wars puzzle in this ever-expanding universe; they don't need to be born into a quasi-royal bloodline.

But Frog Lady is an exception. Never tell us Frog Lady's name. She is forever Frog Lady. If we're talking about quasi-royal bloodlines, let's start the Frog Lady bloodline. Let's truly hope to the Star Wars gods that 30 years from now we're talking about the long, storied legacy of Frog Lady, and how it started at the back end of 2020—a year that truly needed, and truly benefitted from, the presence of Frog Lady.
