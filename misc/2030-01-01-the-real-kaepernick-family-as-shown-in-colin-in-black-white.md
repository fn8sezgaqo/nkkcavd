# The Real Kaepernick Family as Shown in ‘Colin in Black & White’

Regardless of your politics, regardless of how well you think Colin Kaepernick’s NFL career would have gone following his release from the 49ers in 2017, Kaepernick was, objectively, a college phenom. But as dominant as Kaepernick proved to be in college—over 10,000 passing yards and 4,000 rushing yards in his four years with Nevada Wolf Pack—he was only offered one scholarship late into his senior year of high school. The University of Nevada, Reno was the only school willing to give him a shot.

The new Netflix series, Colin in Black & White (created by Ava DuVernay and Kaepernick) dramatizes Kaepernick’s high school career, culminating in the moment he was finally offered a QB spot in a NCAA division 1 program, Nevada. The series—part conventional retelling, part meta and cultural commentary on Blackness in America—portrays real moments from Kaepernick’s teens and strongly features both of Kaepernick’s parents, Rick and Teresa Kaepernick.

Though the events are dramatized, the people and places and feelings are autobiographical; we are inside Kaepernick’s memories. While many, including DuVernay, anticipated the story moving into his adult life, Kaepernick wanted to focus on his teens.

“The reason we focused on high school years is because they are pivotal in figuring out who you are, where you fit within society, and ultimately what your trajectory is going to be,” Kaepernick explained. “It allowed us to dive into the complex racial dynamics of my family, my relationships and society.”

Michael was 17 when he auditioned and played Kaepernick—portraying him between the ages of 14 and 18. Michael told EW that he rarely did team sports while in school, and that he had to learn baseball, football, and basketball during preparation. He said he wanted to bring Kaepernick as close to teenage viewers as he could: “He's a normal teenager facing unreal circumstances. And those circumstances built the legend that we see today. The way that I try to conceptualize it is, he’s you.”

Rick Kaepernick / Nick Offerman

Offerman wrote on Twitter that he was “powerfully chuffed to get to work with my heroes [DuVernay and Kaepernick].” Rick and Teresa Kaepernick adopted Colin when he was 5 weeks old; they had been unable to adopt another child at the time. The family later moved from Fond du Lac, Wisconsin, to Turlock, California, where the entirety of the Netflix series is set.

Teresa Kaepernick / Mary-Louise Parker

Both Teresa and Rick Kaepernick have been vocal about their son’s depiction by news media. After an article fixated on Kaepernick’s tattoos around the time of his first NFL start, Teresa told USA Today, “It annoyed me. You are categorizing this kid on something like tattoos? Really?… That’s how you’re going to define this kid? It’s pretty irritating, but it is what it is.”
