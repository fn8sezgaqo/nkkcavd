# Netflix’s ‘Untold’ Finds the Real Culprit of the NBA's Malice at the Palace

If you’re as obsessed with Netflix’s top ten chart as I am, you’d know that Untold: Malice at the Palace is trending. The first part of the streamer’s five-part series, which primarily looks at controversial topics of sports past, focuses on the infamous Malice at the Palace NBA brawl.

Now, it’s been, ahem, few years since the event took place, so here’s a refresher: in 2004, a fight broke out between members of the 2004 Indiana Pacers and fans of the Detroit Pistons. Fighting in sports is fairly common, what with egos, macho chest pounding, and such. But this event? Fight is too nice of a word to describe what happened during that 2004 NBA game.

Millions of dollars were lost, several key players were suspended, fans were arrested, and the 2004 NBA season was never the same (hence the Malice at the Palace being a key cultural moment to examine).

[NBA's Malice](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/)

What’s great about Untold is that it explains the brawl from different angles, introducing untold storylines as a way to explain the events taking place. So, with Malice at the Palace, fans are getting an inside look as to why and how the fight began in the first place, including the culprit behind the most infamous NBA brawl. His name? John Green.

[of the NBA's](https://980187.com/83u2i7n/)

Who is John Green?

Many blamed Ron Artest, the Pacers’ forward, for starting the brawl. But those who really know what happened (i.e. by watching the documentary), know that the starter of the fight was John Green.

Who is John Green? A fan. Really.

He was just a fan who was a little too invested in the teams’ rivalry. And, once he threw a drink from the stands at Artest, who was lying on the scorers table, all hell broke out. Though Artest and other various players were punished, Green (eventually) got his due too—he was later discovered by the former Oakland County prosecutor, David Gorcya, who sentenced him to 30 days in prison along with 2 years of probation and a lifelong ban to home Pistons games.

Though a fan starting the infamous 2004 NBA brawl isn’t surprising, what is shocking, however, is the unlikely friendship that’s developed between Artest and Green. After the NBA player reached out to Green on Twitter (why? We don’t know), the two grew to like each other, which is kinda weird, especially since Green seems to have no remorse for his actions. But, it has been more than 15 years since the fight, so maybe it’s bygones be bygones.
