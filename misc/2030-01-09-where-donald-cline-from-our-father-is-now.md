# Where Donald Cline From ‘Our Father’ Is Now

Netflix’s latest addition to its basement of true crime bodies, Our Father, continues the streaming network’s recent foray into non-murder iterations of the genre—following recent entries like The College Admissions Scandal, The Tinder Swindler, and Trust No One: The Hung for the Crypto King. Killers are back page stories; this year is all about the scammer.

Our Father recounts the case of Indianapolis doctor Donald Cline, who, over the course of several years, repeatedly used his own sperm to impregnate dozens of unwitting women seeking fertility treatment. While never prosecuted as a crime, such actions are viewed now by victims and medical practitioners as tantamount to malpractice, fraud, and sexual assault. Cline himself, whose religious convictions and affiliations, victims believe, border on eugenics, was not interviewed for the documentary—an absence that brings those familiar with the narrative no closer to understanding its central motives. Still, the impact on those affected by Cline’s deception remains telling. As one victim’s daughter explained during an interview for the documentary: “It’s disgusting to sit there and lay in bed at night and wonder if the person that created you is some racist bigot, and he used my mom as a pawn. And he did it over and over and over again.”

Cline opened his clinic in 1979, when infertility practice was still a new—and relatively unregulated—medical specialty. Some doctors told patients not to inform the children for fear of psychological damage, and a 1977 survey that over half of infertility doctors didn’t keep records, meaning the identity of donor’s was often unknown.

At first, Cline claimed that he was using medical residents as donors, and that he would use each donor for only three successful pregnancies. According to DNA data collected through 23andMe, Cline used one donor at least eight times, resulting in eight biological siblings born between 1979 and 1986.

One of these siblings, Jacoba Ballard, started to piece together the deception after she began looking for her half siblings. She had known since she was 10 that she was the child of a donor. When she began connecting with her siblings, she realized their mothers had also been treated by Cline. Then the siblings took the DNA tests. They believed the donor was not a single medical resident, but rather Cline.

According to victims and state prosecutors, Cline informed some mothers that he was using their husband’s sperm, making the discovery of Cline’s actions especially damaging for families who believed their child to be their own biological offspring—and for those children who believed their father to be their biological father.

As more people began using at-home DNA tests, Ballard’s connections grew. She began reaching out to each one. Ballard and others then filed a complaint against Cline with Indiana’s attorney general, and eventually investigators questioned Cline, who denied he used his own sperm when treating patients.

The documentary points out that there is no federal law to make illicit donor insemination illegal. In addition to Cline, the documentary claims, there are 44 other doctors who have used their own sperm to impregnate their patients; they, too, were discovered through at-home DNA testing.

Where Is Donald Cline Now?

Though Cline could not be charged with a crime related to his medical practice, he was charged with two felony obstruction of justice charges—resulting from lies he told investigators when asked if he had used his own sperm to impregnate his patients.

Cline’s case did not go to trial, and in December 2017, he pleaded guilty. Cline’s jail sentence of 365 days was suspended. He gave up his medical license, even though it had expired that same year.

Cline, now in his 80s, is retired from medical practice. It’s unclear if he still lives in the Indianapolis area, where at least a dozen of his biological children still reside.
