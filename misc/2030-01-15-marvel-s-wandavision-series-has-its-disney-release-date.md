# Marvel's 'WandaVision' Series Has Its Disney+ Release Date

It was always going to be a long wait between the most recent Marvel Cinematic Universe films, and the beginning of the new Disney+ Marvel limited series, and COVID-19 has helped to make that wait even longer. The Falcon and The Winter Soldier, which was originally planned to be the first MCU series released on Disney+, had its production delayed, and now is expected to debut sometime in the middle of 2021. Luckily for fans everywhere, though, WandaVision, the series centered on Wanda Maximoff/Scarlet Witch (Elizabeth Olsen) and Vision (Paul Bettany) wasn't just on-time—it was ahead of schedule. And despite the coronavirus, it's coming—and coming soon.

The show will pick up with Scarlet Witch and Vision in an interesting place—namely that Vision, well, died in Avengers: Infinity War. Spoiler, we know, but if you haven't seen it and have still read this far, you have no one to blame but yourself. WandaVision, in turn, will play quite a bit with genre, sci-fi, and just generally speaking looks like exactly the kind of weird show we can rally around. Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige himself described the show in quite an intriguing fashion, calling it "a half classic sitcom, half MCU spectacular."

But before the show premieres, you might want some details about the upcoming series. Here's what we know about WandaVision.

The show has a coupls spectacular, genre-bending trailers.

Disney revealed a second trailer for WandaVision during the Marvel portion of its massive Investor's Day presentation. More of what we expected: Paul Bettany, Elizabeth Olsen, and the rest of the cast in what looks to be a serious departure for the MCU—it legitimately looks like more sitcom than action movie, in a format we've really never seen before.

The first trailer for WandaVision debuted during the 2020 Primetime Emmys, and it really made it clear that Marvel was coming through on exactly what Feige promised: the show looks wonderfully weird and sitcommy, while still centering on a pair of Marvel characters who we've always wanted to see more from. It's got a little bit of Pleasantville in there, and probably will have some meta Stranger Than Fiction/Too Many Cooks type stuff going on too.

The trailer's caption tells us in words what we see play out visually. "The series is a blend of classic television and the Marvel Cinematic Universe in which Wanda Maximoff and Vision—two super-powered beings living idealized suburban lives—begin to suspect that everything is not as it seems."

The release date for WandaVision has jumped around a bit. It was originally reported that WandaVision planned to land on Disney+ in 2021, and that The Falcon and The Winter Soldier would be the only Marvel show to premiere in 2020. However, Disney surprise included WandaVision as a 2020 release in a YouTube video about upcoming releases, and the wording for the new trailer only said "Coming Soon." Granted COVID-19 and everything else, though, January 2021 is a perfectly acceptable release date.

WandaVision will also directly connect to the MCU feature films.

Part of that delay, according to an Entertainment Weekly exclusive feature on WandaVision, was a reshuffling within the Marvel world to make sure that everything remained in order and fit, storywise, with regards to future and past films.

Marvel chief Kevin Feige also noted within that piece that WandaVision will lead directly into 2022's long-awaited Doctor Strange sequel, titled Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness. Elizabeth Olsen's Scarlet Witch is a part of that movie's cast, and the limited series will almost certainly lead directly into that film's events.

WandaVision will follow Scarlet Witch and Vision after the events of Avengers: Endgame.

WandaVision primarily takes place after Endgame—however, this would mean that Vision is still dead, so it seems probable that we'll see Wanda/Scarlet Witch explore multiple timelines in order to bring him back. And reportedly, a good chunk of the show will take place in the 1950's, and some of the show's promo photos feature Scarlet Witch and Vision in '50s garb.

The events in WandaVision will directly tie in to the upcoming Dr. Strange movie, titled Dr. Strange in the Multiverse of Madness, and Elizabeth Olsen also explained that the series would "explore why she's known as the Scarlet Witch and how she is Scarlet Witch as opposed to Wanda."
