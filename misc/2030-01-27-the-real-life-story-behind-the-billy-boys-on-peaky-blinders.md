Who were the Billy Boys?

The Billy Boys were a Protestant street gang based in Glasgow during the 1920s and '30s. They were known for their "military style behavior," and as in the show, they often marched in formation, and they composed their own songs and music.

The Billy Boys often clashed with Glasgow's Catholic groups like the Norman Conks, and the two gangs would often get into knife fights. Robert Jeffrey, a Glasgow journalist and author, told the BBC that the Billy Boys mainly aimed to terrify the city's Catholic population, who were mainly Irish immigrants. The gang also ran their entire neighborhood, and they operated protection rackets for political meetings and open-air boxing bouts. At their peak, the Billy Boys numbered about 800.

And as seen in Peaky Blinders, they were involved with Sir Oswald Mosley and his fascist rallies, and they often acted as Mosley's bodyguard. However, there isn't much evidence that they ever clashed with the Peaky Blinders in real life.

What does Billy Boy mean?

The Billy Boys got their name from Dutch Protestant monarch William of Orange, who was also known as King Billy. In 1690, he secured Protestant rule in England, Scotland and Ireland following the Battle of the Boyne.

What is the origin of the Billy Boys' song?

The Rangers were a Protestant soccer team in Glasgow, and when the Billy Boys would attended marches and matches, particularly against the Catholic team Celtic F.C., they would sing the Billy Boys song, and it caught on with Rangers fans who weren't affiliated with the gang.

The song features a line about "being up to our knees in Fenian blood," with "Fenian" being used to describe supporters of Irish nationalism and/or Irish Catholics. There's also a nice lyric about telling their enemies to "surrender or die."

Brian Gleeson plays Jimmy McCavern on Peaky Blinders, and while McCavern wasn't a real person, he's based on the real-life leader of the Billy Boys, Billy Fullerton.

Fullerton's grandson told The Scottish Sun that, "As a lad, it wasn't unusual to see men with missing ears, noses and cut faces...They didn't call him the Razor King for nothing."

Fullerton often gave anti-immigrant speeches, and its rumored that he launched a Glasgow branch of the Ku Klux Klan. Fullerton later died in 1962 at the age of 56.
