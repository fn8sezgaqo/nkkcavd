What Is the Zepbound Shot?

Zepbound (the brand name for tirzepatide, its main active ingredient) is a prescription medication that’s injected once per week. It’s FDA approved for weight loss in adults who have obesity or are considered overweight with at least one weight-related condition, and it’s also FDA approved to treat moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in adults with obesity. In both cases, Zepbound is meant to be used in conjunction with a reduced-calorie diet and increased exercise to achieve results.

Zepbound and other injectable weight loss medications, like Wegovy, are often discussed interchangeably, but they’re not exactly the same. Zepbound’s main active ingredient is tirzepatide, while Wegovy’s is semaglutide. They work slightly differently, and in clinical trials, Zepbound has been shown to be more effective when it comes to weight loss.

Zepbound is available in three injectable forms: a single-dose pen that includes the needle inside, a multi-dose KwikPen that contains four doses, and a single-dose glass vial that requires a needle and syringe.

How Does the Zepbound Shot Work?

Zepbound is a GLP-1 agonist (as is Wegovy), a class of drugs that’s also used to treat type 2 diabetes. It works by activating GLP-1 receptors in your body, which (among other things) slows down digestion, keeping food in your stomach longer. This reduces hunger and helps you feel full for longer, while also decreasing food cravings and food noise. As a result, you may eat less.

Zepbound stands out because its active ingredient, tirzepatide, works on two gut hormones, GLP-1 and GIP (which helps increase insulin sensitivity). By targeting both hormones together, tirzepatide may suppress hunger and support weight loss more effectively than medications that target GLP-1 alone.

To take Zepbound, you self-inject the medication once per week. It takes several weeks for the medication to reach a steady level in your body. A clinician can recommend a step-by-step dosage schedule that increases gradually over time. This means that while some people may experience weight loss fairly quickly, it takes about 4 weeks to have noticeable weight loss.

Zepbound Dosing: How the Schedule Works

Zepbound works on a dosage schedule that starts out low and gradually increases over time under clinician supervision. The recommendations for the once-weekly injection proceed like this:

The starting dose is 2.5 milligrams (mg).

After a minium of 4 weeks, increase to 5 mg.

After at least 4 more weeks, increase to 7.5 mg.

It continues like this, increasing after at least 4 more weeks each step, first to 10 mg, then 12.5 mg, then 15 mg. The low-and-slow dosing schedule is meant to help the body adjust to the medication and help you manage unpleasant potential side effects.

Though these recommendations are the standard, a healthcare professional, such as a Midi clinician, will guide you on your personal titration schedule, based on how your weight loss proceeds and the side effects you experience. You should never increase the dose on your own or ignore your clinician’s advice—otherwise, you could make uncomfortable side effects worse.

Doses should be taken around the same time of day on the same day each week (for example, every Monday morning) to keep drug levels consistent in the body. If you miss a dose, don’t stress. The official recommendation in this case is:

Give yourself the dose as soon as possible within 4 days after the missed dose.

If it’s been more than 4 days, skip the missed dose and just take the next dose on your regularly scheduled day.

If you’re unsure, reach out to your clinician for guidance.

Where Do You Inject Zepbound? Injection Sites and Technique

[What to Expect](https://huanle3.com/9axtdif/)

Generally, you give yourself a Zepbound injection, though you can ask someone else for help. It’s recommended to inject it in your stomach, your thigh, or the back of your upper arm (that one usually requires help). You should rotate your Zepbound injection site each week—this can help reduce your risk of common reactions like bruising, itching, a rash, or a lump. If you always inject in the stomach, change up the exact spot each time.

Injecting is pretty simple, especially with an option like the single-use pen:

Choose your injection site, remove the pen cap, and place the pen tip against the skin.

Unlock the pen, then press and hold it against the skin for 10 seconds.

The needle is so small that you may not even feel it, but when you hear two clicks and see the gray plunger, you know the job’s been done.

With the KwikPen, you do basically the same, but you also need to turn the dose knob to your weekly dose before injecting. And for the single-dose glass vial, you need to use a needle and syringe: You fill the syringe with the correct dose from the vial, then inject it with the needle.

With any type, safe storage is important. Always keep Zepbound in the refrigerator between 36° and 46° F. You can store a single-dose pen or vial at room temperature for up to 21 days, but don’t return it to the refrigerator once it’s been at room temperature. Toss any medication after it’s been at room temperature for 21 days, don’t freeze it, and keep it contained in its original carton. (Multi-dose Kwikpens can last a little longer; discard those after 30 days at room temperature.)

Be sure to dispose of Zepbound vials properly, immediately after each use. Put the pen or needle and syringe in a heavy-duty plastic container with a tightly fitted lid, or even better, an FDA-cleared sharps disposal container.

What to Expect After Your Zepbound Shot

You’ll start to notice the effects of the medication on your appetite pretty quickly. That said, noticeable weight loss can take a few weeks or even months. In the first 4 weeks, you may lose some weight, but the steadiest loss of pounds tends to happen between 3 and 8 months as your dosage increases. The maximum weight loss is usually achieved by 72 weeks, with plateaus typically happening around 60 to 72 weeks. After reaching that point, people primarily use Zepbound to maintain weight loss.

All these results can vary depending on your body’s reaction to the medication and your personal dosage schedule. What you eat and your exercise routine can also affect how much weight you lose and how quickly you lose it.

It’s important to note that Zepbound isn’t meant to be used as just a kickoff to weight loss. It’s intended for long-term, ongoing use, and stopping the medication can lead you to regain the weight you lost. One 2023 clinical trial found that adults who took tirzepatide long term continued to lose some weight, while adults who discontinued use gained weight back.

Other research points to similar findings. For example, in a randomized clinical trial, participants who took tirzepatide lost nearly 21% of their body weight over 36 weeks, then were randomized to continue with their treatment or switch to placebo. A year later, those who stopped taking tirzepatide had regained an average 14% of the weight back. Only about 17% of participants who took the placebo maintained most of their initial weight loss, compared with nearly 90% of participants who continued taking tirzepatide. A healthcare professional, such as a Midi clinician, can help figure out the best treatment plan for you.

Another 2026 study found that weight regain after stopping tirzepatide and similar GLP-1 medications was common, and it happened faster than if the weight loss had resulted from diet and exercise changes alone. Researchers suggested that healthy lifestyle changes are necessary for the long-term success of tirzepatide use.

Once you start taking Zepbound, it’s a good idea to track your symptoms and weight changes and bring them up with your clinician at follow-ups. This can help them make dosage recommendations and let them track whether the drug is working for you.

Zepbound Side Effects and Safety

Zepbound slows down gastric emptying and digestion, which means you’ll feel less hungry and have fewer food cravings. When you do eat, you’ll feel full faster. But it’s true that Zepbound can cause some uncomfortable side effects.

Either the same day you inject it or the following day, you can expect to start feeling some of the following:

Gastrointestinal (GI) side effects: Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain, indigestion, excess burping, and heartburn.

Injection site reactions: You may experience redness, swelling, itching, or a small rash. These reactions should usually last only a few hours or days.

Fatigue: Zepbound can make you feel more tired than usual, especially when you first begin the medication.

Often, these side effects feel the most intense in the first few days after the shot. They should gradually decline as your body adjusts to the dose you’re taking. But when your dose is increased, the effects may feel more intense again as your body adjusts.

Zepbound also comes with the possibility of more serious side effects. These are more rare but still worth noting:

severe gastrointestinal issues, such as gastroparesis (stomach paralysis)

dehydration

gallbladder issues

pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas)

serious allergic reactions (swelling of the face, throat, lips, or tongue; difficulty breathing; severe rash or itching; very rapid heartbeat; feeling faint or dizzy)

hypoglycemia (low blood sugar)

Some evidence in rats exposed to GLP-1 agonists had a higher risk of thyroid tumors and thyroid cancer, but there’s currently no human data that supports this.

If you experience or think you’re experiencing any of the above, be sure to get in touch with your clinician immediately, as you may need to stop the medication. If you have a severe allergic reaction, seek emergency care.

Zepbound is not a fit for everyone. Some people shouldn’t use it, including anyone who:

has a personal or family history with medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)

has multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)

has had a serious allergic reaction to tirzepatide or any of the ingredients in Zepbound

has a history of pancreatitis or severe GI issues, such as IBD or gastroparesis

You also shouldn’t inject Zepbound while pregnant or breastfeeding.

The Zepbound Shot in Midlife and Menopause

For midlife women, Zepbound may seem particularly enticing. Studies have shown that hormonal changes during this time can have a significant effect on body composition. That includes weight gain (particularly around the abdomen) and a loss of muscle mass, which can slow your metabolism and make it harder to lose weight.

That’s where Zepbound comes in: It can make it easier to lose weight when it feels like your body is working against you. And one 2026 study found that being on hormone replacement therapy (HRT, now commonly referred to as menopause hormone therapy, or MHT) along with tirzepatide may make weight loss even more successful.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Is Zepbound the same as Ozempic?

Zepbound and Ozempic are similar but not the same. Zepbound (the branded name for tirzepatide) is a GLP and GIP receptor agonist, which has an effect on two different receptors. Ozempic (the branded name for semaglutide) only has an effect on the GLP-1 receptor.

What should I avoid while taking Zepbound?

You shouldn’t take Zepbound if you’re already taking a GLP-1 or another diabetes medication. Because it slows digestion and has uncomfortable GI side effects, you should avoid foods that are fatty, greasy, spicy, or highly acidic.

How long does it take for a Zepbound shot to kick in?

You can start to feel the effects of Zepbound (such as a reduced appetite or GI side effects) on the first day. But it can take weeks or months to see significant weight loss.
