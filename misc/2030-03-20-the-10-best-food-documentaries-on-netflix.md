# The 10 Best Food Documentaries on Netflix

It wasn't too long ago that the chef was simply the man (and, yes, we mean man) in the back of the kitchen—sweaty, tattooed, probably not worth anyone's attention. But times changed quickly, and now the chef has become a star—TV host, celebrity personality, author, hypebeast god. And with the rise of the chef, comes the rise of his and her product: the food. Yes, if it's cool to cook, it's even cooler to eat, and to know what you're talking about when you chow down.

These documentaries capture the culinary art and hedonistic joy off all things edible. Some profile chefs. Some battle it out between cuisines. All are about more than just food. Throw these on for some cooking inspiration. Here are the best food docs on Netflix right now.

[The Best Food Documentaries](https://beautybiometrics.hbsnzj.com/)

Anthony Bourdain: Parts Unknown

Bourdain's book Kitchen Confidential helped turn chef perception from crook in the kitchen to cultural celebrity. Which Bourdain himself became ... but not as the man in the kitchen. Bourdain saw his role as culinary communicator, and his series Parts Unknown testifies to that legacy. It's also a sharp work of travel journalism. (Some of the best episodes barely even talk about food.)

Jiro Dreams of Sushi

This isn't just one of the best food documentaries on Netflix; it's one of the best documentaries, period. Quiet, exact, mindful, Japanese chef Jiro Ono has probably been making sushi longer than you've been doing literally anything. You watch this film to learn about life. Not just fish.

[The Best Food Documentaries](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/3798700226.html)

Sour Grapes

Crime. Fraud. International intrigue. Sour Grapes is spy thriller of food docs. Never look at wine the same ever again.

Chef's Table

[on Netflix](https://longevity.peppysun.com/article/best-longevity-peptide-research-stacks-2026-aging-pathway-combinations-palmetto-peptides-71a8df1ce6ff/)

If Vivaldi blasting over time-lapsed food service doesn't drive you into the kitchen, nothing will. Each episode is a formidable mini-documentary in its own right. Our favorites include Season 3's Jeong Kwan and Season 5's Christina Martinez.

[Food Documentaries on](https://yldzdh.com/7mqnm7h/)

Ugly Delicious

David Chang hosts this sloppy series (in a good way!), which goes deep into supposedly-simple cuisine and shows how food can be a political, culturally-illuminating product as much as a meal.

Rotten

The moldy, expired insides of food production. Eating can be a choice, and choices have consequences.

Street Food

From the team behind Chef's Table, Street Food expands the definition of chef from a Michelin star to a food cart. The best cooks on the planet don't always take reservations. Sometimes, you just have to show up, line up, and get your grub standing on the street.

Barbecue

Filmmakers travel across the world to explore that one ancient ritual that unites us: grilling with fire.

[Documentaries on Netflix](https://mendstride.snaketongueofficial.com/)

Cowspiracy

If Parasite director Bong Joon-Ho's Okja (also on Netflix) didn't convince you to be more conscientious about the meat you consume, Cowspiracy will certainly stuff questions into your carnivorous head. We're talking about sustainability here.

The Magic Pill

Keto is a sensation. Eating fat is practically a cult. And yet it may just help stave off disease and improve the planet's health? Find out.
