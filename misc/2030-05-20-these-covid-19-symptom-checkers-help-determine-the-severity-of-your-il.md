# These COVID-19 Symptom Checkers Help Determine the Severity of Your Illness

A flurry of online COVID-19 symptom checkers are available to help determine whether a cough or fever is a sign of the disease caused by the new coronavirus. It's important to remember that these tools are helpful but shouldn't be substituted for medical advice, says John Whyte, M.D. and Chief Medical Officer at WebMD.

"I know a lot of people want to use it [trackers] for a quick check on their symptom but symptoms checkers are a starting point—not an end point. Rather they can help give you information for which you can use to talk to your doctor," Dr. Whyte tells Men's Health.

Here's what you should know about three different online trackers:

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

The CDC offers a Coronavirus Self Checker that aims to "help you make decisions about seeking appropriate medical care," according to its website. Launch the tool and a pop-up box leads you through a series of multiple choice questions about your symptoms, your underlying health conditions, and your location. Its questions include things like:

Whether you or a loved one is sick

Geographic location

Gender of sick person

Age of sick person

If the sick person is experiencing life-threatening symptoms such as seizures or slurred speech

If the sick person coughs up blood or has trouble breathing.

After indicating that a person has moderate breathing problems, which is a COVID-19 symptom, but has not been in contact with someone who is sick, the following instructions are given:

"Go to the nearest emergency department. Tell the emergency staff if you have had contact with someone with COVID-19 or if you have recently been to an area where COVID-19 is spreading."

Apple COVID-19 Screening Tool

The Apple screening tool, developed with the CDC, is easier to use than the organization's official tracker. The quiz dedicates one web page to each question, and includes a "back" button, which allows you to re-answer a question if you select the wrong choice. The CDC version does not allow this, meaning you need to start over if a mistake is made. The questions are more detailed than the in the CDC tool.

Doctor On Demand

Some telehealth companies have rolled out their own versions of the CDC tool. However, they have no official ties to the governmental agency. This version by Doctor on Demand was created based on CDC guidelines, according to the company's website.

Similarly to the Apple tracker, a webpage is dedicated to each question, and you have the option to go back and correct responses.
