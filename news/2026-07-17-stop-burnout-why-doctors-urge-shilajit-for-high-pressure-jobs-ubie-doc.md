What Is Shilajit?

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like substance that oozes from rocks in high-altitude mountain ranges—most notably the Himalayas. Used for centuries in Ayurvedic medicine, it's rich in:

Fulvic acid: A powerful antioxidant and carrier molecule that helps your body absorb vitamins and minerals.

Humic acids: Organic compounds that support detoxification and nutrient uptake.

Trace minerals: Over 80 minerals in ionic form, including iron, zinc, magnesium and selenium.

This unique profile makes Shilajit an adaptogen, meaning it helps the body adapt to physical and mental stressors without overstimulation.

How Shilajit Supports Cognitive Performance

Doctors often suggest Shilajit to people in demanding roles—executives, first responders, athletes—because it can address multiple facets of brain health:

Enhanced Mitochondrial FunctionMitochondria are the "powerhouses" of your cells. Fulvic acid in Shilajit helps improve mitochondrial energy production, translating to sharper focus and sustained mental energy during long work hours.

Sharper Memory and LearningSome studies show that Shilajit may support synaptic plasticity (the brain's ability to form new connections). This can lead to better short-term memory recall and quicker problem-solving.

Reduced Mental FatigueBy lowering oxidative stress and inflammation in neural tissues, Shilajit helps clear "brain fog" and keeps you alert when under pressure.

Balanced Mood and Stress ResponseAdaptogenic properties help regulate cortisol (the primary stress hormone). Lower cortisol levels can mean fewer anxiety spikes during important presentations or critical decision-making.

Safe Usage, Dosage and Precautions

Like any supplement, Shilajit must be used responsibly:

Recommended Dosage

Standard dose: 300–500 mg per day of a purified, lab-tested extract.

Timing: Take with breakfast or lunch; avoid late-day use if you're sensitive to stimulants.

Possible Side Effects

Mild digestive upset (nausea, loose stools) in some individuals.

Rare allergic reactions—always start with the lowest effective dose.

Quality Matters

Choose products certified for heavy-metal testing and free of contaminants.

Look for standardized fulvic acid content (ideally 50–60%).

Who Should Be Cautious?

Pregnant or breastfeeding women

People with iron overload disorders (hemochromatosis)

Those taking blood thinners or medications that affect blood sugar

Always consult your healthcare provider before starting any new supplement—especially if you have existing health conditions or take prescription drugs.
