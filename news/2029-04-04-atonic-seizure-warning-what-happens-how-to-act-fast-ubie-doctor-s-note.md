What happens during an atonic seizure?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

An atonic seizure, also known as a "drop attack," is a type of seizure where muscles suddenly lose strength and tone. This causes the person to go limp, their head to drop, or their body to collapse briefly. Atonic seizures typically last less than 15 seconds, and the person usually remains conscious throughout the episode.

Explanation

Atonic seizures, sometimes called "drop attacks," involve a sudden loss of muscle control. Key points include:

Muscle weakness: Muscles suddenly relax, leading to a loss of posture or collapse. Duration: These seizures are usually very brief, lasting only a few seconds. Consciousness: The person may remain awake but may also lose consciousness briefly. Risk of injury: Because of sudden falls, there is a risk of injury, so safety precautions are important. Recovery: After the seizure, muscle strength returns quickly, but the person may feel weak or tired for a short time.
