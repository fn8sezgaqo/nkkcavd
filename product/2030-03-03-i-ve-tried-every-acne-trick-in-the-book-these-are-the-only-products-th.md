AS THE SELF-PROCLAIMED sweatiest man in NYC, I constantly look for ways to make sure my skin doesn’t break out. I wash my face two to three times a day, depending on how grimy I get. My skincare routine stays mostly the same year-round, but I switch to lighter products in the summer and more hydrating ones in the winter. And hey, I’m Beauty Bob, so I better have a clear face, right?

The truth is I’ve mostly got Accutane to thank for that. I took a full course of the oral acne medication as a teenager and it basically reset my skin. It was a 99.9 percent fix for me that has extended, at this point, for decades. But, frustratingly, pimples are not behind me. Breakouts come from a lot of different sources: hormone-driven oil production, clogged pores, bacteria, and inflammation, according to Joshua Zeichner, M.D., director of cosmetic and clinical research in dermatology at Mount Sinai.

Just like my teen years, I’ve tested pretty much every tactic for keeping that occasional flare-up in check. I’ve tried patches, scrubs, spot treatments, facials, at-home lasers, and plenty of products recommended by derms or friends. Some helped. Some flared up my skin more. Now, I have a handful of regular products that help prevent breakouts, plus a few for my “emergency kit” when a random pimple appears out of left field.

One of my best-kept secrets is a random find from a moment of desperation: a sunburn spray called Cool Skin, meant to be used after a beach day. I had grabbed it from the shelf of my beauty editor wife Katie who’d heard it could calm down a breakout. Whenever I feel a zit brewing under the surface, I dab this liquid on and it usually shuts things down before they escalate. Truthfully, I never knew why it worked, so I asked Dr. Zeichner to explain it to me.

He told me this spray is basically an anesthetic for sunburns. It has a small amount of lidocaine that numbs the area, so a painful pimple feels less tender. It also contains a low concentration of lactic acid, which can help loosen dead skin at the surface and start to clear the pores. Dr. Zeichner did, however, make it clear that this is not a true acne treatment. There’s no strong data that it calms inflammation in a meaningful way or treats breakouts the way the tried-and-true acne ingredients, like retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, and salicylic acid, do. Rather, think of it as comfort care: If a zit hurts, this product can take the edge off while your actual acne routine does the heavy lifting.

Instead, Dr. Zeichner says there’s a different ingredient I should check out: hypochlorous acid, which is quietly becoming one of the buzziest ingredients in acne care. Your body actually produces hypochlorous acid on its own as part of your immune response to fight bacteria and reduce inflammation. It’s mostly available in sprays, but it’s also starting to show up in serums and masks designed for sensitive or acne-prone skin. “It has antimicrobial effects and is thought to help lower levels of acne-causing bacteria,” Dr. Zeichner says. He sees it as a smart addition to a routine anti-acne plan, especially post-sweat.

To figure out what your skin needs, it’s helpful to know how acne works in the first place. Dr. Zeichner describes the mechanics of acne like this: Your face is filled with thousands of tiny pipes. If you're acne-prone, it’s usually because your skin produces more oil, sheds dead skin cells less efficiently, or harbors higher levels of acne-causing bacteria. That means many of those pipes are already partially clogged. You can’t predict which one will turn into a full-blown pimple, so the best move is prevention by treating your whole face consistently rather than just chasing the spot that’s acting up.

[My Breakouts](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/blog/3526867296.html)

Dr. Zeichner says to stick with noncomedogenic products (a.k.a. products that won’t clog your pores—it’ll say that word right on the label) and start a topical retinoid (ideally 1-2 nights per week in the beginning, then work up to nightly as your skin tolerates it). A topical retinoid like adapalene helps keep breakouts from forming in the first place by acting as your pores’ “pipe cleaner,” he says, and it also happens to be the gold standard to even out texture and smooth fine lines. If your acne has already emerged and it's red and inflamed, reach for a benzoyl peroxide spot treatment. For clogged pores, excess oil, and smaller breakouts, salicylic acid is your best bet. You can use these spot treatments before a breakout comes to the surface too if you know which areas of your face are more acne-prone.
