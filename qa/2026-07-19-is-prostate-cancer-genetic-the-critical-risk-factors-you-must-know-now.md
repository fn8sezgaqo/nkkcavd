# Is Prostate Cancer Genetic? The Critical Risk Factors You Must Know Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/15/2026

Is there a genetic component to prostate cancer risk?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

[Critical Risk Factors You](https://glp-insights.xianfengchem.com/)

Internal Medicine

Prostate cancer does have a genetic component and can run in families. Men with a father or brother diagnosed with prostate cancer face roughly double the average risk, and that risk climbs higher when multiple relatives are affected or when diagnoses occurred before age 65. Inherited gene mutations—such as BRCA1, BRCA2, HOXB13, and Lynch syndrome-related genes—can also raise susceptibility.

However, family history is only one piece of the picture. Lifestyle, age, ethnicity, and current symptoms all play a role in your overall risk. If you're noticing changes like frequent urination, difficulty starting or stopping urine flow, pelvic discomfort, or blood in urine, it's worth investigating sooner rather than later. A free, instant, online symptom check can help you understand what your symptoms may mean, whether they align with prostate concerns or something else entirely, and guide your next steps—so you can approach your doctor with clarity and confidence.
