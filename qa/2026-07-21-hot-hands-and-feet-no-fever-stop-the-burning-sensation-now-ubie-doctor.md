Hot hands and feet without a fever are often caused by how your body regulates blood flow and temperature—not an infection. Common triggers include nerve sensitivity, hormonal shifts, circulation changes, or environmental factors. Simple adjustments like improving sleep quality, wearing breathable clothing, staying hydrated, and keeping your environment cool can help ease these sensations.

When your hands and feet feel hot but you do not have a fever, it might be because of changes in how your body moves blood around. Research shows that factors like sleep loss can make your feet warmer even when your hands stay cooler. Your nervous system has an important role in managing this by directing blood where it is needed, and sometimes this can cause one part of your body to feel warmer than another.

To help address these sensations, try to get regular, restful sleep since being well-rested can help your body regulate its temperature better. It might also help to wear light, breathable clothing and keep your living space at a comfortable temperature. Some people find that relaxation techniques, like gentle breathing or stretching, can calm the nerves and improve blood flow.
