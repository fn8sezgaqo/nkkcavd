What causes knee pain when bending and straightening the leg?

Internal Medicine

Knee pain when bending and straightening is commonly caused by issues with the tissues around the knee, including cartilage, tendons, ligaments, or muscles. It can also result from conditions like osteoarthritis, patellofemoral pain syndrome (problems with kneecap tracking), meniscus tears, or tendonitis. Identifying the underlying cause is essential for effective treatment and preventing further joint damage.

Explanation

When you bend or straighten your knee, many parts have to work together. Sometimes, the pain happens because the cartilage that cushions your joint is worn away, as seen in arthritis. This makes the surfaces of your knee rub together, causing pain. The tendons that connect your muscles to your knee, like the quadriceps or patellar tendon, can also become irritated or inflamed (a condition called tendonitis).

Another common cause is an issue with how the patella, or kneecap, moves within its track. If it doesn't move smoothly, it can rub against the bone and hurt.
