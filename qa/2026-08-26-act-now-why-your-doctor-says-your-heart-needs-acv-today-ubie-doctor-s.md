What the Research Shows

Several studies and meta-analyses have explored ACV's impact on heart-related measures:

In small clinical trials, people consuming 1–2 tablespoons of ACV daily experienced modest drops in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure over 8–12 weeks.

Animal studies suggest acetic acid can help reduce total and "bad" LDL cholesterol while boosting "good" HDL cholesterol.

Preliminary human research links ACV intake to lower levels of inflammatory markers (e.g., C-reactive protein), which play a role in atherosclerosis.

ACV slows carbohydrate digestion, helping prevent spikes in blood sugar—another contributor to heart disease long-term.

While larger-scale human trials are needed, the existing data are promising enough that many clinicians include ACV as part of a heart-healthy regimen.

Blood Pressure Reduction

How to Use ACV Safely

To get the most benefit while minimizing risks:

Always dilute ACV. Mix 1–2 tablespoons (15–30 mL) into a large glass of water.

Drink it before or during meals to help blunt carbohydrate absorption.

Start with smaller amounts (1 teaspoon) and build up as tolerated.

Consider taking it through a straw to protect tooth enamel.

Rinse your mouth with plain water afterward.

Precautions & When to Consult Your Doctor

ACV is generally safe for most people, but be aware of:

Digestive irritation: If you have acid reflux or an ulcer, check with your doctor first.

Medication interactions: ACV may affect how certain drugs work (e.g., diuretics, insulin).

Low potassium: Excessive use can lower potassium levels, leading to muscle cramps.

Dental health: Undiluted vinegar can erode enamel over time.

If you experience new or worsening symptoms—such as chest pain, significant lightheadedness or severe digestive pain—stop ACV and speak to a doctor immediately. Other Proven Ways to Lower Cardiac Risk

While ACV can be a helpful addition, it works best alongside other heart-healthy habits:

Balanced diet: Emphasize vegetables, fruit, whole grains, lean proteins and healthy fats (e.g., olive oil, nuts).

Regular exercise: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week.

Weight management: Losing as little as 5–10% of body weight can improve blood pressure and cholesterol.

Smoking cessation: Quitting smoking cuts heart disease risk by about half within a year.

Stress management: Techniques like meditation, yoga or deep breathing support healthy blood pressure.

Conclusion

Apple cider vinegar offers a low-cost, generally safe way to support heart-healthy changes—particularly in lowering blood pressure, improving cholesterol and reducing inflammation. However, it's not a substitute for prescribed medications or a balanced lifestyle. Before making ACV a daily habit, discuss it with your doctor, especially if you take medications or have existing health issues.
