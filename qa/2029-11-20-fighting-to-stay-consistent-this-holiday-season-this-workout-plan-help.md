# Fighting to Stay Consistent This Holiday Season? This Workout Plan Helps You Master the Lean-Bulk.

A CALORIE SURPLUS isn’t always a bad thing. Do it right and you can juice your activity level while giving your body the raw materials to build more muscle. This concept is at the heart of an oft-misunderstood idea known as a “lean bulk.”

What’s a lean bulk? Essentially, it’s a teensy surplus designed to help you pack on muscle with minimal fat. Typically, a bodybuilder may eat 500 (or sometimes more!) calories above maintenance every day for several months to add size. The downside: They’re adding a blend of muscle and flab. During a lean bulk, you eat just 200 to 300 calories above maintenance. This surplus isn’t significant enough to pack serious size onto your frame, but it can earn you some muscle. The key: You need to push your limits with a solid strength program—and get plenty of ancillary activity.

You’ll pursue both goals with an eight-week program that’s built for office-party/visit-the-family/watch-TV season. Twice a week, you’ll hit the weights hard. You’ll also get an anytime-anywhere circuit; do it as many times as you can (even multiple times a day!) to build cardio fitness and challenge underrated movement patterns.

It’s the perfect mental liberator for the holiday season—and it’ll have you primed with new strength for a big 2026 of workouts too. Your goal is to train at least four days a week. Twice a week, you’ll do your Total-Body Strength session, resting at least two days between each. On at least two other days (but more than that if you can), grit out the Quick Circuit. One other day a week, take a 10- or 15-minute run.

PLANK WALKOUT

Start standing. Bend at the waist and place your hands on the floor. Slowly walk your hands out until you’re in pushup position; tighten your abs and keep your hips and shoulders square to the floor as you do this. Pause, then walk your hands back to your feet. Stand and squeeze your glutes. That’s 1 rep.

WALKING LUNGE

Start standing, a kettlebell held at your right shoulder. Step your right foot forward, then lower into a lunge. Drive off your left foot and step forward into another lunge. Continue taking lunge steps forward until time is up. Tight on space? Take as many lunge steps forward as you can, then turn around and continue doing reps. Work for 40 seconds, then rest for 20 seconds on each side.

PUSHUP TO SHOULDER-TAP

Start in pushup position, hands shoulder-width apart, abs and glutes tight. Bend at the shoulders and elbows, lowering until your chest is an inch from the floor, then press back up. Keeping your hips square to the floor, touch your right hand to your left shoulder. Return to pushup position; do another rep, this time touching your left hand to your right shoulder. That’s 1 rep.

SINGLE-ARM ROW TO CLEAN

Stand holding a kettlebell in your right hand, feet slightly wider than shoulder width. Keeping your abs tight, push your butt back slightly, lowering your torso so it’s at a 45-degree angle with the floor. This is the start. Row the bell back toward your hip; lower with control. KETTLEBELL SWING

Stand three feet in front of a kettlebell. Push your butt back and bend at your hips just slightly. Keeping your core tight, grasp the bell with both hands. Explosively pull the bell back between your legs, then stand and squeeze your glutes; this will drive the bell forward. As the bell reaches its apex, let it swing back between your legs, starting the next swing. That’s 1 rep.

SPIDERMAN LUNGE

Start in pushup position, then shift your right foot just outside your right hand, keeping your left leg straight. Squeeze your left glute. Raise your right hand toward the ceiling, following it with your eyes. Reverse the moves back to pushup position, then repeat on the other side. That’s 1 rep; do reps for 60 seconds. Do 2 sets.

BULGARIAN POSITION RDL

Stand holding dumbbells at your sides, your right instep on a bench. Keeping the dumbbells close to your left shin, slowly push your butt toward the bench and lower your torso toward the floor. Stop when you feel your hamstrings tighten or your back rounds, whichever comes first. Stand and squeeze your glutes. That’s 1 rep; do 3 sets of 8 to 10 per side.

DUMBBELL ROW

Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, a dumbbell in your right hand. Hinge forward and place your left hand on a bench, box, or chair for support. Keeping your hips and shoulders square to the floor, pull the dumbbell upward toward the right side of your rib cage. Pause, then lower. That’s 1 rep. Do 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps per side in the first month. Do 3 sets of 10 to 12 reps in the second month.

INCLINE BENCH PRESS

Lie with your back on a bench set to a 30-degree incline, hands holding dumbbells directly over your shoulders, abs and glutes tight. Squeeze your shoulder blades. Bend at the elbows and shoulders, lowering the dumbbells to within an inch of your chest. Hold for 2 seconds, then press back up. That’s 1 rep. Do 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps.

DUMBBELL THRUSTER

Stand holding dumbbells at your shoulders, abs and glutes tight. Push your butt back slightly, then bend at the knees, lowering your torso until your thighs are parallel to the floor. Stand explosively and press the dumbbells overhead. Lower to your shoulders. That’s 1 rep; do reps for 40 seconds. Then rest for 60 seconds. Do 3 sets.

HOW YOU CAN EXPLOIT THE CALORIC CUSHION

MH nutrition advisor Dezi Abeyta, RDN, shares a gift for your hungry muscles.

THIS ISN’T THE time to chase PRs. Your current goal is different: Pull back from training intensity just slightly and eat in a small calorie surplus. By staying in what nutritionists call the caloric cushion (100 to 200 extra calories above maintenance a day), you give your body just enough fuel to protect your muscle while letting training dip.

How? Research shows that mild overfeeding can trigger an increase in low-key movement (think: fidgeting, walking a bit more, or pacing around the kitchen). That adds up to 200 to 300 extra calories burned daily—and keeps you leaner than you think. Just follow these three rules to live in this caloric cushion.

FIBER UP.

Shoot for 30 to 38 grams of fiber daily. You know the drill: Veggies (like roasted roots), legumes, and berries get you there, helping slow digestion and prevent the eat-then-crash spiral.

PACK PROTEIN.

Hit 30 to 40 grams of protein every meal to stay full, balance your blood sugar, and keep fueling your muscles. Think: tart cherry smoothies, turkey breast, or salmon.

ENJOY SOME TREATS.

But do it smart. You can hit the dessert trays as long as you pair your fun eats with protein (which will naturally make you eat less).

LEAD WITH WATER.
