# What Is Imperforate Hymen? Symptoms, Causes, Diagnosis, Treatment, and Prevention

What Is Imperforate Hymen? Symptoms, Causes, Diagnosis, Treatment, and Prevention

An imperforate hymen is a condition in which the hymen covers the entire opening of the vagina, sealing it shut.

The condition is a congenital disorder, meaning that it’s something a girl is born with. Experts are not sure why it happens, notes MedlinePlus.

The hymen is a thin membrane that usually covers part of the opening of the vagina. It typically has an opening that widens as a girl approaches puberty.

Hymens may have different shapes, with the most common shape being a crescent moon, according to the Cleveland Clinic.

The presence of a hymen doesn’t indicate “virginity.” In fact, some girls are born without a hymen.

The hymen can stretch or tear during sexual intercourse, tampon insertion, sporting activities, or medical procedures, notes the Cleveland Clinic.

Signs and Symptoms of Imperforate Hymen

Doctors often diagnose imperforate hymen in girls shortly after birth, but some girls may not know they have it until puberty.

In a pubescent girl, lack of a first menstrual period may be a sign of an imperforate hymen.

An imperforate hymen typically doesn’t cause any problems until a girl gets her period. At this point, the hymen membrane can block menstrual blood from flowing out of the vagina, notes MedlinePlus.

This blockage may cause:

A painful mass or a feeling of fullness in the lower abdomen or pelvis (from the buildup of menstrual blood in the vagina)

How Is Imperforate Hymen Diagnosed?

An imperforate hymen can often be diagnosed with a simple pelvic exam.

During a pelvic exam, your doctor may have you lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet up on a pair of supports. The doctor will inspect your genitals for any abnormalities.

If you appear to have an imperforate hymen, your doctor may recommend some additional tests — such as a pelvic ultrasound — to make sure there aren’t other problems contributing to your symptoms, MedlinePlus.

Treatment and Medication Options for Imperforate Hymen

An imperforate hymen can be fixed with a minor surgical procedure.

Surgery to remove an imperforate hymen can be performed in infants in some cases, but it is more typically done closer to puberty, according to MedlinePlus. An imperforate hymen may also resolve on its own at puberty, when estrogen levels increase.

For the surgery, the doctor will make a small cut in the hymen to remove the extra tissue and create a normal-sized opening for the vagina. If there’s old menstrual blood stuck in the vagina, the doctor will remove it at this time.

In some cases, the doctor may insert a small ring at the entrance of the vagina to prevent the opening from closing. But most patients just need to apply topical emollients or a small amount of topical estrogen for a short period after the surgery, according to StatPearls.

Girls typically recover from the surgery within a few days.

After the surgery, you’ll be able to have normal menstrual periods and can use tampons.

Surgery to remove an imperforate hymen won’t affect a girl’s future ability to have vaginal intercourse or have children.
