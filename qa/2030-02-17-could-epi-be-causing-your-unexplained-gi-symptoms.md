# Could EPI Be Causing Your Unexplained GI Symptoms?

Could EPI Be Causing Your Unexplained GI Symptoms?

If you’re plagued by mysterious gas, bloating, and diarrhea, you might have exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI), which occurs when the pancreas lacks the enzymes to properly digest the food you eat. You’re more at risk of EPI if you have certain health conditions, including chronic pancreatitis, celiac disease, diabetes, cystic fibrosis, or pancreatic cancer, says Steven Gillon, DO, a specialist in digestive diseases at Holy Name Medical Center in Teaneck, New Jersey.

Another risk factor is pancreatic surgery: According to a review published in April 2020 in the World Journal of Gastrointestinal Pathophysiology, EPI is a common complication of pancreatic surgery that is often underdiagnosed and undertreated. People who have a history of alcohol abuse are also at higher risk of EPI.

Doctors likely don’t think of EPI as often as they should, according to Dr. Gillon. Because EPI and other digestive conditions share some symptoms, it can be tricky to diagnose. But, getting the correct diagnosis is important. EPI can cause symptoms that can have a negative impact on quality of life and lead to potentially life-threatening complications, such as malnutrition and cardiovascular disease, according to research.

If your doctor suspects you may have EPI, they will likely take blood and stool samples and order tests, according to the World Journal of Gastrointestinal Pathophysiology review.

Something else to watch for is steatorrhea, which are foul-smelling, oily stools (which float, stick to the sides of the toilet bowl, and are hard to flush) that can be especially worse after eating fatty foods. This is a specific sign of EPI, and you should notify your doctor if you experience it, says Khalid Khan, MD, a gastroenterologist and the medical director of the Islet Cell Transplant Program at MedStar Georgetown Transplant Institute in Washington, DC.

Abdominal Pain

Abdominal pain is another symptom of many gastrointestinal problems, as is bloating in the abdomen. With EPI, you’ll feel that pain more when you eat, especially with fatty meals, Dr. Lebwohl says. The symptoms of diarrhea and stomach pain are also associated with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), a more common condition than EPI. In younger people, these symptoms are more likely to be IBS, Dr. Khan says, but for people 60 or older, particularly if they have diabetes, EPI is a greater possibility.

Unexplained Weight Loss and Fatigue

If you find yourself losing pounds and you’re not sure why, plus you have stomach pain and fatigue, discuss the possibility of EPI with your doctor, Gillon says. EPI prevents proper digestion of food, so people with EPI typically need to eat a nutrient-rich diet as part of their treatment, along with pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy, according to The National Pancreas Foundation (NPF).

Vitamin Deficiency

If you have EPI, your body doesn’t properly digest food, so you’ll have trouble absorbing certain nutrients, including the key fat-absorbable vitamins A, D, E, and K, Gillon says. That’s why some people with EPI experience bone loss ( osteoporosis) and other health issues related to a lack of these vitamins, according to a review published in February 2023 in the World Journal of Gastroenterology. In fact, many people with EPI need supplements to help maintain appropriate levels of these vitamins, says the NPF.

When to Call a Doctor

If you’re experiencing a combination of common EPI symptoms, call your doctor. Before your appointment, track your symptoms — when they occur and if they’ve changed over time. Remember that fat in your stools is the classic symptom of EPI, as opposed to other digestive conditions. “It’s important to be mindful of your symptoms and not ignore or minimize them if there’s a significant change,” Lebwohl says. “If a person is at increased risk of EPI, diagnosis and treatment can result in a major improvement in quality of life.” In addition to treatment with PERT and vitamin supplements, your doctor can also help you make healthier lifestyle choices, such as improving your diet, avoiding alcohol, and not smoking.
