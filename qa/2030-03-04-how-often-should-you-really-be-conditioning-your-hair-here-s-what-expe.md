Treat your hair like your skin—one product simply can’t do it all. Shampoo is your cleanser, washing away the grime. Conditioner is your serum or moisturizer in one, restoring balance and keeping things smooth, so that your hair looks shinier and more alive. In short, the question isn’t if men should be using conditioner, it’s how often.

Why It Matters

I don’t think many people are going to argue against the need to use shampoo regularly, but conditioner is just as important. While shampoo’s primary function is to cleanse,oil, and other buildup off the scalp and out of strands, conditioner then soothes and hydrates.

Dermatologist Dr. Viktoryia Kazlosukaya, MD, explains that conditioner smooths and protects hair from damage, helping to prevent breakage. Without it, hair may feel dry, rough, or lifeless. Beyond the health of your hair, skipping conditioner will make it look worse and more difficult to style. Conditioner softens your hair, making it easier to brush, says Emmy-nominated hairstylist Dean Banowetz. It also adds moisture and shine, reduces frizz and breakage, and prevents your hair from feeling dry and rough.

And while shampooing needs to be a mandatory habit for the sake of keeping your hair, scalp, and relationships healthy, it strips all or most of the sebum from your scalp (which you need some of), so you need to follow with conditioner to rehydrate it. Conditioners then use lipids and humectants to restore moisture and smooth the cuticle, says dermatologist Dr. Maryam Safaee, MD.

How Often Should the Average Guy Condition His Hair?

Just like how often to wash your hair, there is no universal number for every guy. What’s more important is that you’re doing it regularly, as it will quickly become noticeable if you do not. As Dr. Safaee explains, conditioner smooths and shields your hair, making it easier to manage and less prone to damage. Skip it, and you’re left with strands that feel dry, rough, and dull.

The simplest guideline is that most men should be conditioning after every time they wash their hair. This will replenish the moisture stripped by shampoo, smoothing the hair cuticles and protecting strands from dryness and breakage. But there is also risk in overdoing it. Dr. Safaee recommends conditioning no more than thrice weekly, as over-conditioning can weigh hair down—an issue which is particularly problematic for short, fine hair.

Dr. Kazlosukaya also subscribes to a less-is-more approach, warning of the buildup it can cause. However, she notes that for those with long hair, it requires additional care, which can include daily conditioning. She points to TikToker Orlando James as someone who would need to condition daily, thanks to his long, luscious curls.

Similarly, those with color-treated or dry hair, Banowetz backs the condition every time you wash theory. Celebrity barber and RED Pro ambassador Joshua Meekins adds that it can also be beneficial to condition on days you don’t wash to help refresh hair, tame frizz, and rebalance the scalp, especially after a workout or a sweaty day. Ultimately, the two biggest factors are hair type and lifestyle.

What Role Does Hair Type Play?

If you’re an active guy, you should be washing your hair more often than other, less sweaty dudes, so you need to compensate with conditioner to avoid drying out the scalp and strands. Here, follow the condition after every wash rule. Different hair types and conditions are where it gets a bit tricky. For example, if you have thin, fine hair, limit conditioning to three times a week to keep it light, and avoid heavy formulas with oils or butters that can weigh hair down and leave it looking greasy, says Dr. Kazlosukaya.

When dealing with conditions like dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis, or psoriasis, she says applying conditioner directly to the scalp can sometimes worsen scalp issues by causing buildup. But using it only on the hair shafts and ends to moisturize is typically safe and won’t cause issues, so that’ll be your best strategy. Meekins confirms that fine and straight hair should focus on lightweight formulas, using them less often. However, coarse or curly textures need more moisture, so heavier, hydrating conditioners will be best, and you’ll need to use them more often, he says.
